# The Predictive Brain

**Question:** Is perception passive reception or active inference?

<!-- more -->

**Research Abstract:**
Predictive processing and the free-energy principle model cognition as minimising prediction error / surprise through perception and action. This reframes “experience” as controlled model-maintenance under uncertainty.

**Anchor Refs:**
Friston, “The free-energy principle” (2010); predictive processing literature.

**Additional Notes**
1. World Models (The "Physicalist" Path) - Also covered in Body-Schema paradox essay
Lead by figures like Yann LeCun, this paradigm argues that AGI must be "grounded" in the physical world.
The Theory: To have common sense, an AI must understand physics (gravity, causality, persistence).
Why it’s AGI-focused: It moves away from language as the primary source of truth. By training on video and sensory data, the goal is a "Foundation Model of Reality" that can plan and navigate the world as a toddler does, rather than just talking about it.
2. System 2 Reasoning (The "Systematic" Path)
Inspired by Daniel Kahneman’s "Thinking, Fast and Slow," companies like OpenAI (with o1/Strawberry) and Google DeepMind are moving toward models that can "think" before they speak.
The Theory: Current LLMs are "System 1" (fast, intuitive, prone to error). AGI requires "System 2" (slow, deliberate, logical).
Why it’s AGI-focused: This uses techniques like Test-Time Compute and Chain-of-Thought. Instead of immediate output, the model explores a "tree" of possible solutions, checks its own logic, and discards errors. It turns the model from a database into a reasoning engine.

**Story Beat:**
Now we can say what “being in a world” computationally means: continual prediction under constraint.
